Unit 1: Basic Economic Concepts

Production Possibilities Curve (PPC)

Representation:Maximum combinations given fixed resources and technology
Points:Points on curve = efficient and feasible
Points inside curve = inefficient
Points outside curve = unattainable (unless exploit)
Slope of curveStraight line = constant opportunity cost
Concave = increasing opportunity cost
Assumptions:1. Economy only produces 2 goods
2. Resources fixed (labor, land, entrepreneurship, capital)
3. Technology stays constant (no advancement)
4. Resources used in their full potential
Consumer
goods		Created for direct consumption (food)
Capital goodsCreated for indirect consumption, used to make other goods
(machines)
Economic
growth		Capital goods production > Consumer goods production
=more economic growth
Movement of
points in graph		Movement along curve = change in allocation of resources, does
not change the curve
Movement from inside to edge = decrease in unemployment,
increase efficiency, net economic growth
Problems and
limitation		1. Oversimplification, where there should be more than 2 goods
IRL
2. Resources are fixed, where there should be flux IRL
3. Technology is constant, where it shouldn’t IRL

Comparative & Absolute Advantage

DefinitionComparative = Producing at lower opportunity cost than another
country
Absolute = can produce more products overall
ExampleCountry A produce 100 monster and 50 redbull
Country B produce 10 monster and 30 redbull
Country A has absolute advantage in production
Country B has comparative advantage in producing redbull
(1 redbull = 1/3 monster in Country B,
1 redbull = 2 monster in Country A)
SpecializationCountry B should specialize in producing redbull
Price of
trading		In acceptable range of the 2 opportunity costs
E.g. 1 redbull should be sold for between ⅓ to 2 monsters
More than ⅓ , less than 2

Demand

Law of DemandPrice increase = quantity demanded decrease
MisconceptionDemand = slope of the curve
Quantity demanded = point on the curve
PRICE INCREASE DOES NOT LEAD TO DEMAND DECREASE
Determinants /
shifters of demand
curve		1. Tastes / preferences
2. Income (normal goods vs inferior goods) [prefer normal
goods when income increase]
3. Prices of related goods (Substitutes & complements)
4. Population
5. Expectations of future prices / income

Supply

Law of supplyPrice increase = quantity supplied increase
Misconception
(same)		Supply = the slope of the graph
Quantity supplied = a point on the graph
PRICE INCREASE DOES NOT LEAD TO SUPPlY INCREASE
Determinants /
shifters of supply		1. Input / resource / production costs
2. Technology advancement
3. Number of sellers
4. Taxes, subsidies
5. Expectations of future prices
6. Prices of related outputs (multi-product firms)

Price ceiling

DefinitionLegal maximum price set below equilibrium price
EffectLow legal price = quantity demanded increase = quantity supplied
falls
Less producers are willing to produce at lower sales price
Leads to SHORTAGE

Price floor

DefinitionLegal minimum price above the equilibrium price
EffectHigh legal prices = quantity supplied increase = quantity demand
falls
Same opposite reason as price ceiling
Leads to SURPLUS

Unit 2: Economic Indicators and the Business Cycle

Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

DefinitionMarket value of all final goods and services produced
Intermediate
good		Good used to produce another good, not the final product
Avoided counting intermediate good since it will lead to double
counting
Expenditure
approach		GDP = C + I + G + X
C= consumer spending
I = investment spending
G = government spending
X = net export
Income
approach		GDP as sum of wages, rent, interest and profits
MeasurementMeasures domestic production

Real GDP

DefinitionTotal value of all final goods and services produced, adjusted for
inflation
CharacteristicConstant prices from base year
Nominal GDPUses prices of the current year
ExampleYear 1: Monster costs $5, 10 monsters were sold
Year 2 : Monster costs $6, 10 monsters were sold
Nominal GDP Year 1 = $50
Nominal GDP Year 2 = $60
Real GDP (Year 1 = base year) = $50
MeaningIncrease in nominal GDP makes it seem like there is an economic
growth
However, Real GDP = Nominal GDP, indicating 0 growth

GDP per capita

DefinitionMeasure of country’s economic output per person
CharacteristicShows standard of living, long term growth is defined as sustained
increase in Real GDP per capita

Limitations of GDP

Cannot be
measured by
GDP		1. Income distribution
2. Underground / illegal production
3. Living standards

Difference between nominal GDP, real GDP & GDP per capita

Nominal GDPMeasures production of goods and services within nation’s
borders
Increase output / increase price of goods and services produced
= increase nominal GDP
Distorted by inflation
Real GDPMeasures production but price stays constant
Price change cannot affect real GDP, only change in output can
cause real GDP to change
Removes inflation effect
GDP per capitaProduction per person
Can show the standard of living of individuals

Unemployment

Frictional
unemployment		Short term, between jobs, entering labor forces
Structural
unemployment		Skill mismatch, technology changes, industry decline
Cyclical
unemployment		Economic downturns, lack of demand
Actual rate vs
natural rate		actual rate unemployment > natural rate unemployment
= recessionary gap
= potential GDP > actual GDP (shows underperformance)
= a point inside PPC curve
Natural rate only has Frictional and Structural unemployment
Actual rate has all 3 unemployments
Actual Rate > Natural Rate = positive cyclical unemployment
exists = economic downturn exists

Cost of inflation

DefinitionInefficiency and economic distortion created by rising prices
Shoe-leather
costs		People trying to counter effects of inflation (transactions avoid holding
cash)
This waste valuable time and resources, which could have been used
productively somewhere else
Menu CostsCost of changing prices to firms
Firms change prices to keep up with inflation (retagging items,
updating digital menus)
This consumes resources (labour, materials)

Cost of inflation

WInner of
inflation		Borrowers
They pay back loans with cheaper dollars with less purchasing
powers than they borrowed
Looser of
inflation		1. Lenders
Money received has less purchasing power than money lent out
2. Fixed income earners
Their income stays same while prices of products they buy goes up
Anticipated
inflation		People knows inflation is coming
Make adjustments (bank raise interest rates)
Cost of inflation will usually be shoe-leader cost and menu cost
Unanticipated
inflation		People dont know inflation is coming
Much more damage dealt due to less adjustments can be made in
time
Leads to winner and losers of inflation

Business Cycles and Output Gaps

ExpansionReal GDP increasing = unemployment decreasing = inflation may
rise
recessionReal GDP decreasing / below potential GDP = unemployment
increasing = lower inflation = deflation may rise
Potential outputLevel of real GDP when economy is at natural rate of
unemployment
Output gapRecessionary gap = actual real GDP < potential GDP
Inflationary gap = actual real GDP > potential GDP

Consumer Price Index (CPI)

FormulaTotal cost this year / total cost base year x 100%
CharacteristicIgnore price changes for nonconsumer items
Increasing in price of nonconsumer items can increase GDP
deflator but does not affect CPI
Inflation rateChange in CPI = inflation rate

GDP Deflator

FormulaNominal GDP / Real GDP x 100%
CharacteristicIgnored import prices
Increase import prices can cause CPI to rise but does not affect
GDP deflator
Inflation rateChange in GDP deflator = inflation rate

Unit 3: National Income and Price Determination

Aggregate Demand Model

DefinitionTotal demand for all finished goods produced
FormulaC + I + G + X
C = consumer price
I = investment spending
G = government spending
X = net export
SlopeDownward slope
Relationship
to GDP		AD = GDP in the long run
Real Wealth
Effect (C)		Decrease in overall price = increase purchase power = increase
consumption = increase quantity of real GDP demanded
Interest Rate
Effect (I)		Decrease price level = less demand for money = decrease interest rate
= increase investment spending = increase quantity of good demanded
Net Export
Effect (X)		Price level decrease = domestic goods are cheaper for foreigners =
export increase = import decrease = increase in net export = increase
demand for domestic production
Shift of AD
curve		1. Changes in the components of formula
2. Interest rates
3. taxes

Short Run Aggregate Supply Model

SlopeUpward sloping due to sticky wages
Sticky wageTendency of nominal wages to adjust slowly to changes in economy
Nominal wage is the unadjusted salary of workers
Cause of
sticky wage		1. Workers have fixed wage contracts, prevent immediate
adjustment
2. Minimum wage prevents wages from dropping below the line
Impact of
Sticky wage		Increasing price level = decrease purchasing power of workers
Labour becomes cheaper, firms hire more workers to increase output
= positive slope of SRAS
Shift of
SRAS		1. Increase productivity = increase SRAS
2. Positive supply shock = lower input price/technological
advancement = increase SRAS
3. Negative supply shock = increase input prices = decrease
SRAS
StagflationDecrease in aggregate supply
Caused by increase in resource price

Long Run Aggregate Supply Model

SlopeVertical slope
Impact of
sticky wage		No impact in the long run
Wages and prices become flexible as contracts expire
Economy returns to potential output
Shifts of
LRAS		Changes in resource and technology

Output Gaps

Negative
output gap		Recessionary Gap
- Actual output < potential output
- Leftward shift of LRAS
- High unemployment
Positive
output gap		Inflationary Gap
- Actual output > potential output
- Low unemployment
Long run self
adjustment		Recessionary Gap:
High unemployment = drop in wages = SRAS shifts rightward, back to
full employment
Inflationary Gap:
Low unemployment = wages increase = SRAS shifts leftward, back to
full employment

Multipliers

Marginal
propensity to
consume
(MPC)		Change in consumption / change in income
Marginal
propensity to
save (MPS)		Change in saving / change in income
FormulaMPS + MPC = 1
Spending
multiplier		1 / (1-MPC) = 1 / MPS
AD change = change in Government spending x spending multiplier
AD change = change in tax x MPC x spending multiplier
Tax multiplier-MPC / (1- MPC)
Negative since increase tax = decrease aggregate demand

Fiscal Policy

Discretionary
fiscal policy		Government changing spending / tax to close output gap
Shift ONLY the AD curve left / right
Expansionary
fiscal policy		Implemented for recessionary gap
Increasing government spending + decreasing tax = increase AD
Government NEVER shifts SRAS / LRAS to close recessionary gap
Contractionary
fiscal policy		Implemented for inflationary gap
Decreasing government spending + increasing tax = decreasing AD
Government NEVER shifts SRAS / LRAS to close inflationary gap
Automatic
stabilizer		Recession: tax falls = transfer payment increase = cushion fall in AD
Expansions: tax rise = transfer payment decrease = restrains rise in
AD
Limits of
Fiscal Policy		1. recognition , decision, implementation lag
2. Government raise interest rates, reducing investment
3. Changes equilibrium price

High liquidity = cash & demand deposits
Low liquidity = stocks, long term bonds

High risk = individual stocks
Low risk = government bonds / bank deposits
Rate of

Relationships Between Financial Assets

Rate of returnsHigher risk = higher rate of return
Higher liquidity = lower rate of returns

New bonds are at higher current rate = more attractive
Old bonds at lower current rate = must lower the price for
competitiveness
Cash / Demand
deposits		- Highest liquidity
- Lowest risk
- Lowest rate of return (often 0%)
Bonds- Moderate liquidity
- Moderate risk
- Moderate rate of return

Relationships Between Financial Assets

Stocks- Moderate liquidity
- Highest risk
- Highest rate of return
Different assetsFirms hold different assets to balance immediate operational needs
(high liquidity) with the goal of maximizing future profits (less
opportunity cost and higher rate of return)

Short Run Philips Curve (SRPC)

DefinitionInverse relationship between inflation rate and unemployment rate
in the short run
SlopeDownard slope
Movement along
SRPC		- Increase AD (inflationary gap) = moving up (higher inflation,
lower unemployment)
- Decrease AD (recessionary gap) = moving down (lower
inflation, higher unemployment)
Shifting SRPCAffected by SRAS ONLY
Decrease SRAS = stagflation / supply shock = rightward SRPC

Long Run Philips Curve (LRPC)

DefinitionNo trade off between inflation rate and unemployment rate in long
run
slopeVertical slope at natural rate of unemployment (NRU)
Shifts of LRPCOnly shifts if there is change in NRU
- Labour force characteristics
- technology

Gaps

Inflationary
gap		Unemployment < NRU
SRPC on the left of LRPC
Recessionary
gap		Unemployment > NRU
SRPC on the right of LRPC

Quantity Theory of Money

FormulaMV = PY
M = money supply
V = velocity of money
P = price level
Y = real output
Long run
effect		V and Y are stable / constant
Increase money growth = increase inflation
Nominal vs
real variable		Nominal = measured in current market price
Real = adjusted for inflation, reflect actual purchasing power
Real variable = (Normal Variable / price index)(100%)
The Fisher
Effect		Nominal interest rate = Real Interest Rate + Expected Inflation
In the long run, nominal interest rate increase but real interest rate
remain the same due to money growth = higher inflation
HyperinflationV rapidly increase during hyperinflation (act as multiplier for inflation)
- People rush to spend money on real assets
High M and high V lead to high P
