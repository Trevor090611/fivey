Unit 3: Work, Energy, and Power

3.1 Translational kinetic energy

DefinitionK = ½mv². Kinetic energy is a scalar and depends on the speed measured in the chosen reference frame. Doubling speed quadruples K.
UnitsMeasure mass in kilograms and speed in meters per second. Energy is measured in joules (J).

3.2 Work and changes in kinetic energy

Constant forceW = Fd cos θ, where θ is the angle between the force and displacement. Work is positive for a force component along the displacement, negative for one against it, and zero for a perpendicular force.
Variable forceFind the signed area under the graph of the force component along the direction of motion versus position.
Work–energy theoremNet work on an object equals its change in translational kinetic energy: Wₙₑₜ = ΔK.

3.3 Potential energy

SystemPotential energy belongs to a system of interacting objects. For a conservative force, W = −ΔU. The change in U is independent of the path.
GravityNear Earth’s surface, ΔU = mgΔh. For two masses separated by distance r, U = −Gm₁m₂/r with zero at infinite separation.
SpringUₛ = ½kx², with x measured from the spring’s unstretched length. Doubling the stretch or compression quadruples Uₛ.

3.4 Conservation and changes in energy

Mechanical energyMechanical energy = K + U. When no energy is transferred or converted out of mechanical forms, a loss of potential energy is balanced by a gain in kinetic energy, and vice versa.
System boundaryStart by choosing the objects in the system. Energy can cross its boundary through work or other transfers. Track those transfers and all relevant forms of energy.
FrictionMechanical energy can decrease as thermal energy increases. For an isolated system that includes the rubbing surfaces, total energy is conserved.

3.5 Power

Average powerP = ΔE/Δt, or W/Δt when the energy transfer is work. One watt equals one joule per second.
Instantaneous powerFor a force at angle θ to the velocity, P = Fv cos θ. At constant force along the motion, power increases with speed.
