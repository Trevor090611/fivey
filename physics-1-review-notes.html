Unit 1: Kinematics

1.1 Scalars, vectors, and signs

Scalars and vectorsDistance and speed have magnitude only. Displacement, velocity, and acceleration also have direction. Choose a positive axis, then add signed components.

1.2 Motion with constant acceleration

DefinitionsDisplacement Δx = x − x₀. Average velocity is Δx/Δt; average speed is total distance/Δt. Average acceleration is Δv/Δt.
EquationsFor constant a: v = v₀ + at; Δx = v₀t + ½at²; v² = v₀² + 2aΔx. In free fall with upward positive, a = −g even at the highest point.

1.3 Reading motion graphs

Slopes and areasSlope of x versus t gives velocity; slope of v versus t gives acceleration. Signed area under v versus t gives displacement; signed area under a versus t gives change in velocity.
Speeding upSpeed increases when v and a have the same sign and decreases when they have opposite signs.

1.4 Relative velocity

Changing observersv(object relative to observer) = v(object relative to ground) − v(observer relative to ground). Observers moving at a constant velocity relative to each other measure the same acceleration of the same object.

1.5 Two-dimensional motion

ComponentsFor a vector of magnitude v at angle θ above +x, vₓ = v cos θ and vᵧ = v sin θ. Treat perpendicular components separately using the same time interval.
Projectile motionWith negligible drag, aₓ = 0 and aᵧ = −g. For a launch and landing at equal heights, time of flight is 2v₀ᵧ/g and horizontal range is v₀ₓ times that time.

2.2 Forces and free-body diagrams

Draw forcesChoose the object or system. Draw only external forces acting on it, then resolve vectors along convenient axes. Weight points toward Earth; a normal force is perpendicular to the surface; a string’s tension pulls along the string.

2.3 Newton’s third law

Force pairsA’s force on B equals B’s force on A in magnitude and is opposite in direction. The two forces act on different objects. Weight and a surface’s normal force on the same object are not a third-law pair.

2.4–2.5 Inertia and net force

Zero net forceAn object with zero net force stays at rest or moves at constant velocity. Zero velocity at an instant, by itself, says nothing about net force.
Per-axis equationsΣFₓ = maₓ and ΣFᵧ = maᵧ. On an incline of angle θ, weight has components mg sin θ down the slope and mg cos θ into it. Find the normal force from the perpendicular force equation.

GravityF_g = Gm₁m₂/r² with center-to-center separation r. The field magnitude a distance r from a spherical mass M is g = GM/r² outside that mass.
Scale readingsFor a rider supported only by a floor and gravity, with upward positive, N − mg = ma. In free fall N = 0 while gravity still acts.

2.7 How to calculate friction

Static and kineticStatic friction adjusts up to fₛ,max = μₛN and prevents relative slipping. Once surfaces slide, fₖ = μₖN in the model, opposite the relative sliding direction.

2.8 Spring forces

Hooke’s lawFor an ideal spring, Fₓ = −kx, where x is measured from its relaxed position. The minus sign points the force toward the relaxed position. A force-versus-extension graph of the magnitude has slope k.

2.9 Circular motion

Radial accelerationaᵣ = v²/r = ω²r points inward. The radial component of the actual net force equals maᵣ. “Centripetal” describes that component, such as tension or gravity, rather than an extra force.
Period and directionFor uniform circular motion, v = 2πr/T. Velocity is tangent, so the object accelerates even at constant speed.

Unit 3: Work, Energy, and Power

3.1 Translational kinetic energy

DefinitionK = ½mv². Kinetic energy is a scalar and depends on the speed measured in the chosen reference frame. Doubling speed quadruples K.
UnitsMeasure mass in kilograms and speed in meters per second. Energy is measured in joules (J).

3.2 Work and changes in kinetic energy

Constant forceW = Fd cos θ, where θ is the angle between the force and displacement. Work is positive for a force component along the displacement, negative for one against it, and zero for a perpendicular force.
Variable forceFind the signed area under the graph of the force component along the direction of motion versus position.
Work–energy theoremNet work on an object equals its change in translational kinetic energy: Wₙₑₜ = ΔK.

3.3 Potential energy

SystemPotential energy belongs to a system of interacting objects. For a conservative force, W = −ΔU. The change in U is independent of the path.
GravityNear Earth’s surface, ΔU = mgΔh. For two masses separated by distance r, U = −Gm₁m₂/r with zero at infinite separation.
SpringUₛ = ½kx², with x measured from the spring’s unstretched length. Doubling the stretch or compression quadruples Uₛ.

3.4 Conservation and changes in energy

Mechanical energyMechanical energy = K + U. When no energy is transferred or converted out of mechanical forms, a loss of potential energy is balanced by a gain in kinetic energy, and vice versa.
System boundaryStart by choosing the objects in the system. Energy can cross its boundary through work or other transfers. Track those transfers and all relevant forms of energy.
FrictionMechanical energy can decrease as thermal energy increases. For an isolated system that includes the rubbing surfaces, total energy is conserved.

3.5 Power

Average powerP = ΔE/Δt, or W/Δt when the energy transfer is work. One watt equals one joule per second.
Instantaneous powerFor a force at angle θ to the velocity, P = Fv cos θ. At constant force along the motion, power increases with speed.

Unit 4: Linear Momentum

4.1 Momentum as a vector

Definitionp = mv. Add momentum components with signs. SI units are kg·m/s, equivalent to N·s.

4.2 Impulse and change in momentum

Force and timeJ = Δp = F_avgΔt, or the signed area under a force-versus-time graph. For the same momentum change, increasing stopping time reduces average force magnitude.

4.3 Momentum conservation

Choose the systemTotal momentum is conserved along a direction when net external impulse along that direction is zero or negligible. This applies to collisions, recoil, and explosions even when kinetic energy changes.

4.4 Solving collisions

Objects stickFor two objects that stick in one dimension, v_final = (m₁v₁ + m₂v₂)/(m₁ + m₂) when external impulse is negligible. For an elastic collision, also equate total kinetic energy before and after.

Unit 5: Torque and Rotational Dynamics

5.1 Angular kinematics

Displacement, speed, accelerationUse radians and choose a positive rotation direction. With constant α: ω = ω₀ + αt; Δθ = ω₀t + ½αt²; ω² = ω₀² + 2αΔθ.

5.2 Angular and linear quantities

Distance and accelerationAt distance r from a fixed axis, s = rθ, v_t = rω, a_t = rα, and aᵣ = rω². All points on a rigid object share ω, but points farther from the axis move faster.

5.3 Torque about a pivot

Magnitude and signτ = rF sin θ = F times the perpendicular lever arm. A force whose line of action passes through the pivot produces zero torque. Assign opposite signs to clockwise and counterclockwise torques.

5.4 Calculating rotational inertia

Mass distributionFor point masses, I = Σmᵢrᵢ² using perpendicular distances to the chosen axis. In the parallel-axis relation, d is the separation between the two parallel axes; it need not equal a radius.

5.5 Rotational equilibrium

Balance conditionsΣτ = 0 means constant angular velocity. For a stationary extended object, set both ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0. Choosing a pivot at an unknown force’s line of action can eliminate that force from the torque equation.

5.6 Torque and angular acceleration

Fixed axisFor a rigid body with constant I, Στ = Iα. For a given net torque, a larger I produces a smaller angular acceleration.

Unit 6: Energy and Momentum of Rotating Systems

6.1–6.2 Rotational energy and work

Rotational kinetic energyK_rot = ½Iω². A translating and rotating rigid body has K_total = ½Mv_CM² + ½I_CMω².
Rotational workFor constant torque, W = τΔθ. Net work changes rotational kinetic energy for rotation about a fixed axis. Area under the torque-versus-angle curve also gives work; instantaneous rotational power is τω.

6.3–6.4 Angular momentum and impulse

Angular momentumFor a body rotating about a fixed axis, L = Iω. For a particle about a point, magnitude L = rp sin θ. Angular impulse equals ΔL and is the area under a net torque-versus-time graph.
ConservationIf external net torque about the axis is zero, total L remains constant. A skater drawing arms inward increases ω as I falls; rotational kinetic energy can change because the skater does internal work.

6.5 Rolling constraints

No slipFor a wheel on a fixed surface, v_CM = Rω and a_CM = Rα along the rolling direction. Both v_CM and ω can change as a wheel rolls down a ramp.
SlipA slipping wheel can still rotate; the contact point moves relative to the surface, so the no-slip equation need not hold. Kinetic friction opposes relative motion at that point.

6.6 Circular orbits

Orbital speed and energyGravity supplies the radial force, so v = √(GM/r) for a circular orbit. K = GMm/(2r), U = −GMm/r, and E = −GMm/(2r). Larger circular orbits have lower speed and less negative total energy.
Period and escapeT² = 4π²r³/(GM) for circular orbits. To just escape with zero speed infinitely far away, initial total energy must be zero, giving v_escape = √(2GM/r).

Unit 8: Fluids

8.1 Density of fluids

DefinitionsDensity ρ = m/V, usually in kg/m³. Liquids and gases can flow and have no fixed shape. The ideal-fluid model assumes incompressibility and negligible viscosity.

Gauge Pressure

Pressure exerted on incompressible fluid = constant total volume = constant density
Object underwater experiences pressure from the weight of fluid above the object
- Independent of shape
- Only dependent on depth of submergence

Pressure Difference

Pressure at top of block: ρgh + atmospheric pressure
Pressure at bottom of block: ρg(h+L) + atmospheric pressure

8.2 Pressure and hydraulic systems

PressureP = F_perpendicular/A. In a fluid at rest with constant density, P_absolute = P_surface + ρgh and gauge pressure relative to the surface is ρgh. At the same depth in the same connected static liquid, pressure is equal.
Pascal’s principleAn applied pressure change is transmitted through a confined fluid. For ideal pistons at equal heights, F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂. A larger output force trades off against a shorter piston travel.

8.4 Continuity and Bernoulli

ContinuityVolume flow rate Q = Av. Steady incompressible flow in a full pipe obeys A₁v₁ = A₂v₂, so fluid speeds up through a narrower section.
Energy along a streamlineFor steady, ideal flow, P + ρgy + ½ρv² is constant along a streamline. At equal heights in the same streamline, higher speed corresponds to lower static pressure.
Torricelli’s resultFor a small opening a vertical distance h below the surface of a large open tank, v_exit ≈ √(2gh) when both points are at atmospheric pressure and the surface speed is negligible.
